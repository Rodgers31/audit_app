## JWKS behavior and practical bounds

The combined decoder retains key-derived ES256/RS256 verification, separately configured HS256 secret handling, private-JWK rejection, the 16-KiB token/256-character key-ID ceilings, one-hour valid-key TTL and shared monotonic 30-second attempt budget. Malformed provider responses do not overwrite good state. A failed attempt consumes the cooldown, so recovery/key rotation can wait up to 30 seconds. Cold/expired requests during an in-flight attempt or cooldown fail closed with temporary 503. This deliberate behavior avoids occupying synchronous workers waiting for the leader.

JWKS transport requests `Accept-Encoding: identity`, refuses any nonidentity encoding, follows no redirects, checks declared lengths, caps the accumulated body at 64 KiB and validates at most 20 key objects before publication. The cap protects accumulation/JSON parsing; it is **not a strict socket-byte cap**. A real 4-MiB test observed 81,920 bytes pulled before refusal because HTTPX can supply an oversized next chunk. Elapsed checks run between raw chunks with five-second per-I/O timeouts; they do not promise hard cancellation of DNS/OS scheduling or every blocking operation at an exact wall-clock instant. Every response context closes and every leader flag clears on failure. A provider that disregards the identity-encoding request is refused rather than decompressed.
